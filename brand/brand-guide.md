# CommonWealth — brand guide

CommonWealth is a coalition rewards program for independent, locally owned businesses: shoppers earn points at any member business and spend them at any other. It is operated by **Common Cents Collective**, which appears as the endorser. The brand is bold, does good, and earns trust by being plain about how it works.

## Brand architecture

- **CommonWealth** is the consumer brand. It leads on the app, storefront decals, cards, social and campaigns.
- **Common Cents Collective** is the operating entity and endorser. It appears as "by Common Cents Collective" under the CommonWealth lockup, in footers, terms, merchant agreements and anywhere legal identity matters.
- The endorser never outranks CommonWealth in consumer materials. In merchant-facing and legal materials, the Common Cents Collective lockup may lead.
- Write the brand as **CommonWealth**, one word with a capital W, every time. "CmnWlth" is allowed only for social handles and short URLs, never in running text or signage.

## The idea

Common sense, common cents, the common good. Money spent at a neighborhood shop keeps circulating in the neighborhood, and CommonWealth makes that visible and rewarding. The mark is a coin with a storefront awning inside it: small business and shared value in one shape.

## Voice

Bold, warm, supportive, and straight with people.

- Do: lead with what the shopper gets and where they can use it. Use short sentences and active verbs. Name real shops and neighborhoods. Explain points, value and expiration in plain numbers.
- Do: celebrate the collective ("your neighborhood," "our member shops").
- Don't: hype, fine-print surprises, guilt about shopping at big chains, or financial language that implies points are cash or a bank account.
- Example headline: "Spend here. Earn everywhere." Example UI: "You earned 120 points at Rosa's Bakery. Use them at any member shop."

## Color

Evergreen (`evergreen`) and copper (`copper`) are the brand pair; marigold (`marigold`) is the awning and the one bright signal. Paper (`paper`) is the ground, never pure white.

- Primary buttons: `evergreen` fill with `on-evergreen` label. Secondary: `copper` fill with `on-copper`.
- Earned-points figures and links: `copper` text on `paper` or `surface-raised`.
- Marigold is a fill for badges, bonus events and the awning. Text on it is `on-marigold`; marigold is never used as text on paper.
- Tinted panels (balance cards, merchant callouts): `evergreen-soft` behind `ink` or `evergreen` text.
- All text pairs meet 4.5:1 in both themes. Focus is a 2px `focus-ring`, offset 2px.

## Typography

- **Bricolage Grotesque** (display, 800 and 600) for headlines and the wordmark. Tight tracking, sentence case.
- **Public Sans** (text, 400 and 600) for everything people read. Public Sans was designed for civic services, which suits a program asking for trust.
- Never set body copy in Bricolage, and never letterspace it. Uppercase is reserved for `caption` eyebrows and the endorser line.

## Logo

See the Logos group for files and rules. In short: use the full lockup (mark, wordmark and endorser) by default; the mark alone for app icons and small spaces; keep clear space equal to the coin's rim-to-awning height on every side; minimum mark size 24px on screen, 0.375 in. in print.

## Graphic motif

The awning stripe, marigold stripes with scalloped ends at a 32px pitch (`radius-lg` scallops), is the one supporting pattern. Use it on storefront decals, campaign borders and the member card. Pair it with solid color blocks; never use gradients, drop shadows or photos under the logo.

## Imagery

Real member shops and their owners, natural light, hands and counters, the transaction moment. No stock "diverse group smiling at a phone," no coins or cash raining, no piggy banks.

## Terminology

- The program: CommonWealth. The operator: Common Cents Collective. Participating businesses: member shops. People: members.
- Points: "points" (for example, "120 points"). If the team adopts "Cents" as the branded unit, confirm first that it fits the program terms, since currency-like names can blur the line between points and stored value.
